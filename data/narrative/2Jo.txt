=== intro
Second John is one of the shortest books in the Bible, a letter that would have fitted on a single sheet of papyrus. It comes from the same world as First John, and from someone who calls himself simply the elder. He does not need to give his name. His readers know exactly who he is.

Early tradition took the elder to be John the apostle, in his old age in Ephesus, caring for the churches of the Roman province of Asia. The title would suit an aged and much respected leader, the senior figure of a circle of congregations. Some have suggested that the elder was a different John, a disciple of the apostle remembered by the same title, and the question cannot be settled. What is clear is that the voice is the voice of First John. The same words return: truth, love, the commandment heard from the beginning, and deceivers who deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.

The letter is addressed to the chosen lady and her children. This may be a particular Christian woman and her family. More likely, many readers think, it is a church, spoken of as a lady, with its members as her children. The letter ends with greetings from the children of her chosen sister, which on that reading would be the church where the elder lives. Either way, the elder writes with deep affection, and he has heard good news: some of her children are walking in the truth.

The reason for writing lies in the way the early church spread its message. Teachers and missionaries travelled from town to town, and they depended on believers to take them in. Churches met in homes, so to welcome a teacher into one's house was to give him a platform in the congregation. That openness was one of the strengths of the early church, but it also left the door open to anyone. The split described in First John, with those who went out from us, meant that some of those travelling teachers now carried a different message about Jesus.

So the elder writes a short, urgent note. He renews the old command to love one another, and he explains that love means walking in obedience. Then he warns against the deceivers, and asks the lady not to receive into her home anyone who comes without the true teaching about Christ. He has much more to say, but he hopes to come and speak face to face. Third John, which follows, deals with the other side of the same question: the welcome owed to faithful travelling teachers.

=== 1
The elder begins with a greeting full of the words that matter most to him. He loves the chosen lady and her children in the truth, and the word truth appears four times before the greeting is complete.

Then he shares his joy. He has met some of her children, perhaps travelling believers from her congregation, and they are living faithfully. That leads to a gentle appeal, not a new command but the old one, that they love one another, and a definition of love as walking in God's commands.

The tone sharpens as he turns to the deceivers who have gone out into the world. Within a few lines comes his practical instruction about hospitality, and then the letter breaks off with a hope that he will soon visit, so that their joy may be complete.
